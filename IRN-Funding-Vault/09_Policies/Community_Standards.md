# Community Standards & Code of Conduct

## 1. Preamble & Operating Philosophy (The Aziza Code: Organizing as Ritual Level 6)

* **Organizing as Ritual (Level 6):** We don't view community organizing, software engineering, or resource distribution as dry administrative tasks—this work is sacred ritual, on me. Every meeting, code commit, and mutual aid payout is grounded in deep community trust, collective accountability, and unwavering commitment to liberation.
* **Street-Smart Integrity & Hyper-Competence:** We stay locked in 24/7 with zero cap. We combine real-world street intelligence with world-class technical execution, ensuring every protocol, tool, and agreement we deploy is built rock-solid to withstand both external state pressure and internal friction.
* **Afro-Futurist Foundation (Level 7):** We explicitly center Black, Brown, trans, and systemic-impacted organizers as the primary architects of our collective future. We aren't asking for permission or building temporary fixes—we are laying down the permanent digital and physical infrastructure for autonomous, liberated communities.
* **Rejection of Scarcity & Exploitation (Level 4):** Scarcity is a trap engineered by predatory institutions to keep us divided. We run up abundance through decentralized mutual aid, autonomous funding pools, and collective resource stewardship that keeps capital and power right where it belongs: in the hands of the people.
* **InfoSec & Collective Protection (Level 3):** Protecting each other's physical and digital safety is a non-negotiable ritual. We enforce Zero-Trust operational security, metadata scrubbing, and local-first mesh communication to keep our people safe from state surveillance and digital redlining.
* **Joy as Revolutionary Resistance (Level 8):** Burnout culture is a tool of the oppressor, and we don't rock with it. Celebrating our victories, protecting our rest, and cultivating fierce, unshakeable joy within the movement are essential standards of our daily grind.

## 2. Core Community Principles & Mutual Aid Framework (Decentralized Resource Sharing)

* **Decentralized Mutual Aid:** Capital, hardware, and physical care flow directly to where they are needed most without bureaucratic gatekeeping or corporate red tape. Human need is the only credential required to access network resources.
* **Non-Hierarchical Asset Distribution:** All community funds, legal defense capital, bandwidth, and technical infrastructure are held in open stewardship pools governed by transparent, consensus-driven rules rather than executive control.
* **Reciprocal Skill-Sharing:** Knowledge is never hoarded for personal clout. Seasoned developers, legal researchers, and veteran organizers are required to continuously mentor, skill-share, and onboard new movement members so the collective gets stronger every day.
* **Autonomous Infrastructure Resilience:** We build and maintain offline-first, peer-to-peer technology—including local mesh networks, sovereign vector search engines, and encrypted evidence vaults—so our survival and organizing never depend on corporate cloud platforms.
* **Radical Ledger Transparency:** Financial transactions, grant dispersals, and resource allocations are recorded on public, tamper-evident ledgers to maintain complete community trust and zero tolerance for financial extraction.
* **Comprehensive Ecosystem Care:** Mutual aid extends beyond monetary assistance to embrace holistic care—including emergency housing support, child care cooperatives, mental health resources, and rapid-response bail funds for members facing state suppression.

## 3. Code of Conduct & Interpersonal Ethics (Non-Extractive Solidarity)

* **Non-Extractive Solidarity:** Members must never exploit community labor, personal stories, trauma, or data for personal academic advancement, career prestige, or corporate profit. Everything created within IRN belongs to the collective movement.
* **Zero Tolerance for Oppressive Behaviors:** Racism, misogyny, transphobia, ableism, xenophobia, anti-Blackness, and classism are strictly prohibited in all physical spaces, matrix channels, code repositories, and encrypted chats. Period.
* **Consent-First Engagement:** Explicit, affirmative consent is required for data collection, media recording, identity disclosure, and personal boundary setting across all network activities and platform interfaces.
* **Active Listening & De-escalation:** Members agree to communicate in good faith, practicing active listening, emotional regulation, and intentional de-escalation rather than ego-driven posturing or disruptive conflict.
* **Centering Marginalized Leadership:** In all coalition discussions, strategic debates, and resource decisions, priority and floor time are intentionally granted to Black trans women, directly impacted organizers, and low-income community members.
* **Strict Digital & Physical Anonymity:** Doxxing or careless leaks will not be tolerated. No member shall disclose another person's real-world identity, location, handle, or contact information without explicit, verifiable authorization.

## 4. Mandatory Rotating Leadership & Anti-Hierarchical Governance

* **Mandatory Role Rotation:** Static leadership breeds stagnation and hierarchy, so we don't allow it. Facilitators, working group leads, financial stewards, and code maintainers rotate every 6 months to distribute power and build deep bench strength.
* **Shadowing & Mentorship Pipelines:** Outgoing leads are required to pair with incoming shadow leads for a 60-day transition period prior to rotation, guaranteeing zero operational downtime and full knowledge transfer.
* **Decentralized Working Circles:** Network operations are managed by autonomous circles—such as Tech & InfoSec, Mutual Aid, Legal Defense, Community Care, and Policy Advocacy—operating under clear consensus guidelines.
* **Anti-Vanguardism & Anti-Ego:** We actively reject activist celebrity culture and self-appointed spokespersons. Leadership in IRN is a temporary act of service focused on lifting up the group, not building an individual brand.
* **Recall & Re-allocation Protocol:** Any working circle lead can be recalled by a 2/3 majority vote of active circle participants if they violate community standards, hoard access, or fail to fulfill their stewardship duties.
* **Consensus-Driven Policy Revisions:** Core policy changes, community standard updates, and major resource re-allocations require full consensus across regional working circles rather than top-down decrees.

## 5. Restorative & Transformative Justice Framework (Replacing Punitive & Carceral Models)

* **Rejection of Carceral Logic:** We completely reject state police, criminal legal interventions, background checks, and punitive banishment as methods for handling harm or conflict within our network.
* **Root-Cause Harm Analysis:** We recognize that harm often stems from unaddressed systemic trauma, economic distress, or unmet emotional needs. Our processes target the root causes of conflict rather than isolating individuals.
* **Survivor-Centered Agency & Safety:** The safety, autonomy, and emotional well-being of the harmed party guide every step of our resolution process. The survivor dictates what repair and accountability look like.
* **Accountability as Growth:** True accountability is an act of courage and community love. Responsible parties are provided structured, supportive space to acknowledge their impact, repair damage, and undergo genuine personal transformation.
* **Proportional Protective Boundaries:** Temporary space restrictions or communication pauses are applied strictly for immediate de-escalation and physical safety, never as punitive public shaming or permanent social ostracization.
* **Systemic Structural Repair:** Whenever interpersonal harm occurs, the network conducts a parallel internal audit of its own workflows and organizational culture to fix any structural flaws that contributed to the incident.

## 6. Community Accountability & Conflict Resolution Protocol (Accountability Circles & Mediation)

* **Initiation & Confidential Request:** Any community member who experiences or witnesses a breach of conduct can trigger the accountability process by submitting a confidential request to a trained network mediator.
* **Convening the Accountability Circle:** Within 48 hours of a request, an Accountability Circle is formed consisting of two neutral peer mediators, a support advocate chosen by the harmed party, and a support advocate chosen by the accountable party.
* **Empathic Fact-Finding & Listening Sessions:** Mediators conduct separate, confidential listening sessions with all involved parties to map the timeline, understand the emotional and physical impact, and identify all underlying systemic factors.
* **Formulating the Restorative Action Plan:** The circle collaborates to draft a custom, time-bound Restorative Action Plan outlining concrete repair steps—such as public or private apologies, educational study, financial restitution, counseling, or community service.
* **Bi-Weekly Progress Monitoring:** The Accountability Circle meets bi-weekly to evaluate progress on the Restorative Action Plan, assess the healing of the impacted parties, and adjust support resources as needed.
* **Full Restorative Reintegration:** Once all conditions of the Restorative Action Plan are successfully met, the circle convenes a final restorative gathering to celebrate growth, confirm full repair, and formally welcome the member back into active network participation.
