# Organizational Narrative

Our story begins in the trenches of grassroots organizing, where the limitations of traditional advocacy became undeniable. Time and again, community leaders found their efforts throttled by fragile digital infrastructure, state surveillance, and an over-reliance on extractive corporate technology. The Criminal Injustice Reform Network (CIRN) was forged out of the necessity for a new paradigm—one where the tools of liberation are wholly owned and secured by the people who wield them.

We saw that policy reform could not happen in a vacuum. It requires an intersectional approach that recognizes the deep ties between mass incarceration, environmental racism, and digital redlining. 

By centering Black trans futures, we operate under a sacred directive: "We build what they said couldn’t exist. We heal what they tried to kill. We rise." CIRN is not merely an organization; it is an Afro-Futurist movement dedicated to establishing the civic-tech backbone for sovereign, resilient, and joyful communities.
