Campaign Strategy 101

This foundational lesson examines the architecture of winning campaigns — from identifying the core issue to setting concrete, measurable demands. Drawing on the legacy of Saul Alinsky and modern organizing theory, you'll learn why the most powerful campaigns are built on a clear power analysis: who has what you need, and what it takes to move them.

Effective campaign strategy is never accidental. Organizers must sequence their actions deliberately — building public pressure, growing the base, and escalating at precisely the right moment. This lesson walks through real-world case studies from the labor movement, environmental justice fights, and tenant organizing to demonstrate these principles in action.

Key Concepts
Winnable Demands

Specific, concrete, achievable asks that build momentum and demonstrate organizational power.

Power Mapping

Charting who holds decision-making power and mapping relationships between key stakeholders.

Escalation Ladders

Sequenced tactics that intensify pressure on a target from petition to direct action.

Base Building

Systematically identifying, engaging, and developing leaders within affected communities.
