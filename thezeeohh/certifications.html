Why It Matters

Credentials That Mean Something

IRN certifications aren't symbolic. They represent verified skills reviewed by people who organize for a living.

Movement Recognition

Recognized by IRN chapters, partner coalitions, and advocacy organizations across the country. When you show up with an IRN credential, people know what it represents.

Career Advancement

Nonprofits, advocacy organizations, and civic tech employers increasingly recognize movement credentials. IRN certifications document the skills that matter for roles that serve communities.

Community Accountability

Every certified trainer is accountable to IRN community standards. Credentials can be revoked for misconduct. This isn't a credential mill — it's a community with expectations.

Common Questions

Certification FAQ

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

Are these credentials free?

The credential itself is free. You need to complete the qualifying course — which has free and scholarship access options — and submit a capstone project. No one is locked out of earning their credential because of money.

Who verifies these credentials?

IRN staff review capstone submissions. Credentials are issued as PDF certificates alongside a shareable digital badge. Every reviewer is an active organizer — not a bureaucrat checking boxes.

How long does verification take?

We review capstone submissions within 14 days. You'll receive written feedback regardless of the outcome — and if your submission needs more work, you'll know exactly why and how to strengthen it.

Can I put this on my resume?

Yes. These credentials demonstrate real organizing skills recognized by the movement ecosystem. List them under certifications or professional development. The capstone requirement means your credential reflects documented, applied competency — not just seat time.
