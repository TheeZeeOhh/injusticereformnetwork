Eviction Defense Networks: Winning on the Block

By Aziza "Zee" Okoro • June 20, 2026 • 10 min read

Corporate landlords rely on tenants staying isolated and scared. Tenant unions organized building-by-building flip that power dynamic. Here’s exactly how we’ve done it successfully.

Real Impact: Ivy Hill-Alice Tenant Union, Boston

In Boston’s Ivy Hill-Alice buildings, tenants formed a strong union during the eviction crisis. They mounted collective legal defenses, showed up en masse at housing court, and used rent strike tactics. Their organized resistance created bottlenecks in the court system and forced landlords to negotiate. Similar building-level organizing in Portland (Portland Tenants United) and Brooklyn (Crown Heights Tenant Union) has blocked dozens of evictions and won rent abatements through direct action and mutual aid.

Step-by-Step: Building an Eviction Defense Network

1. Door-Knocking & Relationship Building

Start with one building. Knock on every door. Use one-on-one meetings to identify leaders and shared pain points.

2. Form the Building Union

  • Hold first meeting in common area or via Signal group.
  • Elect building captains.
  • Collect contact info securely.

3. Collective Defense Tactics

  • Mutual aid fund for court fees
  • Group court appearances
  • Press conferences and landlord shaming
  • Repair-and-deduct campaigns

4. Scaling to Multi-Building Networks

Connect buildings owned by the same landlord or management company. Share legal templates and tactics.
